Daisuke Nakama 仲間 大輔

Chief Researcher, Institute for Organizational Behavior Research, Recruit Management Solutions Co., Ltd. (Tokyo, Japan)
Ph.D. in Social Psychology, The University of Tokyo

I study how cooperation and coordination arise and are sustained in organizations and teams. My work combines behavioral experiments, game-theoretic models, agent-based simulation, and analyses of HR and survey data from companies. Recently, I have also become interested in how AI is used in organizations and how it affects the way people work and cooperate.

Research

Team composition and membership fluidity

Teams are made up of members who differ in ability and expertise, and members come and go through transfers and turnover. I examine how such composition and fluidity change members' cooperation and team outcomes, using social dilemma experiments, agent-based simulation, and data from company workplaces.

Representative work:

Organizational design for coordination

How should work be divided when people are uncertain about what others will do? With Yoshio Kamijo, I analyze division of labor and the role of coordinating managers using game-theoretic models and laboratory experiments.

Representative work:

  • Kamijo, Y., & Nakama, D. (2023). Designing division of labor with strategic uncertainty within organizations: Model analysis and a behavioral experiment. Journal of Economics & Management Strategy, 32(2), 257–272. https://doi.org/10.1111/jems.12506
  • Nakama, D., & Kamijo, Y. (2020). Who Should Pay the Coordination Manager?: A Model Analysis of Governance Mechanisms in Role-differentiated Organizations [in Japanese]. Transactions of the Academic Association for Organizational Science, 9(1), 172–177. https://doi.org/10.11207/taaos.9.1_172

Punishment, aggression, and cooperation

The possibility of punishment or attack can sustain cooperation, but it can also create conflict. With Hiroki Ozono, I run experiments on how beliefs about punishment, including fictitious ones, the concentration of the power to punish, and the option to hold weapons shape cooperation and conflict.

Representative work:

  • Ozono, H., & Nakama, D. (2026). Instability of cooperation based on fictitious belief: an experiment with artificial supernatural punishment. Scientific Reports, 16(1), 8244. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-38499-3
  • Ozono, H., & Nakama, D. (2024). I will hold a weapon if you hold one: Experiments of preemptive strike game with possession option. Evolution and Human Behavior, 45(6), 106635. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.evolhumbehav.2024.106635

AI in organizations

How does AI change the way people work and relate to each other in the workplace, and how can it be used in HR research? With colleagues, I study how observing conversations between personalized AI agents affects employees, test whether LLM agents can reproduce types of people in organizations for HRM simulations, and apply natural language processing to text such as 360-degree feedback comments and one-on-one meeting notes.

Representative work:
